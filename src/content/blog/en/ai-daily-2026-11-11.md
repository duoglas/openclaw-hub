## (November 9 backfill) CodeRabbit pairs delivery changes with a code-security product

An OpenAI customer case study published on November 9, 2026 describes CodeRabbit’s use of Codex. CodeRabbit says it shipped roughly 20 times more software over the six months preceding the interview without adding engineers. This is the company’s account of its own delivery changes: the source does not define the unit of “software shipped” or separately quantify the contributions of the models and workflow changes.

For CodeRabbit’s engineering team, the change extended across the delivery process. According to the case study, running multiple agents exposed constraints in local memory and compute, testing, build tooling, and dependencies. The team streamlined releases and reconsidered where human approval belonged. Its stated automation boundary allows CodeRabbit to ship low-risk changes that do not require human intervention; a separate Codex-powered Slack bot triages production incidents, messages, and support tickets and can return a pull request for the team to review and merge. The roughly 20x output claim therefore accompanies a delivery-process redesign rather than serving as an isolated measure of model speed.

For customers, CodeRabbit Secure uses Daybreak with Codex models to find and fix vulnerabilities through security-focused agents. The case study says that during a promotional period described as the preceding two weeks, the company covered $1 million in free deep scans, reporting more than 43,000 vulnerabilities across 13,000 scans. Customers had already fixed thousands of them. These figures describe company-reported scanning activity and remediation progress; the source provides no false-positive rate, severity breakdown, or independent validation, and does not establish whether the offer remains active.

The case study reports that CodeRabbit is used by more than 20,000 organizations and over 150,000 open-source projects. Those figures apply to the platform, not specifically to Secure. For teams using its review and security services, the product expansion brings code-change validation, vulnerability discovery, and remediation within the same business offering. The broader engineering significance is the connection the case draws between increased code generation and changes to review, testing, and approval workflows. The available evidence does not establish that other teams would achieve the same output increase or how Secure compares with competing security tools.
