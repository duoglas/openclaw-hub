## November 17: Loop-9B releases model weights and code

The SenseNova-Loop-9B model card explicitly records the release of model weights and code on November 17 and separately lists the October 31 Preview. Its looped architecture reuses layer weights, accepts text and images, produces text, and specifies a context limit of 512K tokens.

The vendor estimates approximately 4.9 GB for 4-bit model weights, but deployment memory also includes runtime state and the KV cache. At a 64K context, the card estimates about 10 GiB with an 8-bit KV cache or 9 GiB with a 4-bit cache. Weight size therefore does not represent the complete GPU memory requirement.

These are vendor estimates under stated precision and context assumptions. The 512K context limit does not mean that the quoted memory table covers that length. We did not run the model or reproduce benchmark scores, and parameter count alone does not establish superiority over larger models.

[Source S01](<https://huggingface.co/sensenova/SenseNova-Loop-9B>)

## November 17: Separate financial-agent adoption from intentions

The Hong Kong Institute for Monetary and Financial Research released its report on November 17. The announcement hosted by the HKMA says 90% of surveyed financial institutions had adopted or planned to adopt generative AI, compared with 75% in 2025. For agentic AI, 13% had adopted it and another 56% planned to do so.

The report draws on surveys and in-depth interviews commissioned between January and July 2026. The 90% measure combines adoption and intentions; 13% is the stated agentic-AI adoption figure. The survey period also differs from the November publication date and should not be treated as a real-time adoption snapshot.

The announcement identifies explainability and security as challenges and discusses regulatory-compliance uses. It provides bounded survey evidence about institutional demand, without establishing widespread adoption of a particular agent product or a definite business return.

[Source S02](<https://www.hkma.gov.hk/gb_chi/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/11/20261117-3>)

## In the edition's archive: Microsoft's licensed weights retain access conditions

Microsoft's official page archived on November 18 describes MAI Licensed Weights as a way for organizations to deploy and customize Microsoft models in their own Azure environments using proprietary knowledge and workflows. The captured text does not establish an exact publication date; this is a description of the archived documentation, not a launch dated from retrieval time.

The page says customers retain their data and adaptations, with Microsoft using them for training only with express agreement. Customers control deployment, access and upgrades. It also says the offering is coming through Frontier Company engagements and directs readers to their Microsoft account team, rather than offering unrestricted public downloads.

The reported results are evaluations of models adapted for specific customer tasks. Microsoft states that performance and cost advantages vary by workload, configuration and evaluation method. We did not verify contract terms or reproduce those experiments, and the access route does not establish eligibility for every enterprise.

[Source S03](<https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-licensed-weights>)
