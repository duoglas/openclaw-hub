## (November 16 backfill) Overseas data center licensing extends to remote compute access

TechCrunch reports that the US government is preparing new export controls on AI chips and data centers outside the country. Under the interim rule described in the report, companies would need licenses to export, reexport or transfer relevant AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere in the world if those facilities may provide compute to businesses in China or other restricted countries. The reported scope covers data centers or networks capable of supplying more than 12 million total processing performance (TPP). Existing operators providing covered services would have to obtain licenses within 90 days of the rule taking effect; the supplied material gives no effective date from which to calculate that deadline.

The affected parties include chip exporters, data center operators and customers accessing compute through intermediaries or brokers. According to the report, indirect access is also covered, and exporters and operators must monitor customers’ ownership structures and activities to determine whether they are run by entities from the D:5 group of embargoed countries. That group includes China, Russia, Iran, North Korea and 18 others. Compared with the existing chip-shipment licensing requirements described in the report, the change extends scrutiny to whom an overseas facility serves, making the facility’s location only one part of the geographic scope.

The report also identifies new advanced-semiconductor licensing requirements for exports, reexports or transfers to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, while describing an exception in those countries for chips destined for US-based companies. Commercial recommendation systems, such as those used in e-commerce or social media, are reported to be outside the rule’s scope. For the AI infrastructure industry, these provisions describe controls bounded by compute capacity, customer affiliation and use case. They do not establish a blanket prohibition on overseas AI services, and the reported licensing window cannot yet be expressed as a verified calendar deadline.
