## (November 18 backfill) OpenAI: estimated success reaches 46% on two-to-four-hour tasks

In its November 18, 2026 research-acceleration report, OpenAI says estimated autonomous agent success rose from 23% in March to 46% in October for research tasks expected to require two to four hours of human work. For four-to-eight-hour tasks, the estimate rose from 13% to 35%. Results varied substantially by task: 79% for monitoring training runs, 35% for launching them, and 25% for writing inference and serving-stack code. These internal figures document improvement while defining its limits: monitoring results do not establish an equivalent level of automation for inference infrastructure development.

The population directly covered is OpenAI’s own researchers using coding agents. Valued at API list prices, median daily researcher usage reached $2,400 in October, up 62% from September; the 90th percentile reached $23,000, up 105%. Those figures measure usage at list prices, rather than disclosed actual bills. Rising usage coexists with uneven task success, and the report continues to describe humans as central to AI research.

The publication extends OpenAI’s September research-acceleration disclosure with additional quantitative and qualitative measures across research domains. It also reiterates the company’s policy position that AI labs should be required to track and publicly report indicators of recursive self-improvement—the use of AI to accelerate development of future AI systems. That is a corporate transparency proposal, not a newly enacted regulatory requirement. The evidence remains a partial account of changes within OpenAI’s own research process.

## (November 18 backfill) Nvidia: the $105 billion data-center backstop has activation conditions

TechCrunch reports that Nvidia clarified in its quarterly financial commentary that guaranteeing a loan is distinct from providing the financing itself. The report cites OpenAI’s PORTS-Pike data-center campus in Ohio: Nvidia’s backstop totals $105 billion, with OpenAI committing to use Nvidia equipment exclusively at the campus. The arrangement therefore connects the guarantee directly to the customer’s facility construction and equipment procurement.

[Source S02](<https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-clarifies-when-it-will-and-wont-keep-financing-neoclouds>)

## (November 18 backfill) Financing, facility readiness and installation govern the guarantee

The same report cites Colette Kress as saying that the guarantee begins only once the customer has secured the necessary financing, built the facility and installed Nvidia products. If those conditions are not met, the guarantee does not take effect. For data-center developers and financing participants relying on such arrangements, the headline amount alone does not fully describe support during construction; activation conditions are material to its scope.

Kress described this as clarification of existing terms rather than a policy change. TechCrunch, separately, noted that these details had not previously been disclosed publicly. Those statements address different issues: the company denies changing the terms, while the reporting identifies an increase in public disclosure. Neither establishes that Nvidia has withdrawn the guarantee.

The report also says Kress intends to continue supporting some data-center financing while becoming more selective, emphasizing the importance of land, power and building shells. This is a stated intention to screen financing recipients more closely. The supplied material does not identify projects rejected as a result or quantify any realized contraction in financing.
