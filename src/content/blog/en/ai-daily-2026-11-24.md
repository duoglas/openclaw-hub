## November 23: GLM-5.4 subscription access differs from API and weight access

Z.ai's November 23 article describes GLM-5.4's Loop Transformer approach, which increases computational depth by reusing layer weights. A same-day Beijing News Shell Finance report says access had opened to all GLM Coding Plan users.

The archived official page still marked the API as Coming Soon and said model weights would be opened after necessary safety hardening. Subscription access, API access and downloadable weights are distinct availability stages; one does not establish the other two.

The vendor also identified adaptive computation and the relationship between loop count and model scale as continuing research questions. We checked the announcement and access boundaries without reproducing coding benchmarks or treating the architecture description as a performance guarantee for every task.

## In the edition's archive: WALL-OSS robot results do not transfer to Lite simulation weights

X Square's technical article, dated November 23, 2026 and archived on November 24, reports a 55.1% zero-shot success rate for WALL-OSS-1.0 across 312 real-robot tasks freely selected by evaluators. This is a company-reported result. The article date is distinct from first weight availability; the available material does not independently establish when the WALL-OSS and Lite weights first became available.

The public WALL-OSS 1.0 Lite — RoboDojo model card explicitly says those weights were trained for the RoboDojo simulation benchmark and were not validated on real robots. The Lite checkpoint should not be treated as a ready reproduction of the full real-robot evaluation.

The related XR Harness repository labels itself an open-source developer preview with continuing work on capability, performance and reliability. Model weights, task orchestration and real-robot evaluation have separate limits. We did not deploy a robot or perform physical-safety validation.

## Reporting in this edition: 125 ZDR removals do not mean 125 confirmed breaches

Dragos's original investigation says researchers sent approximately two million prompts containing trackable URLs and canary credentials to AI services between July and November 2026. It observed indications of data exposure at at least three providers. That is the investigation window, not a claim that all incidents occurred on one day.

OpenRouter's response records the removal of three providers and withdrawal of Zero Data Retention eligibility from 125 endpoints, including precautionary removals. The count therefore does not establish 125 confirmed leaks or breaches. A separate report attributes to OpenRouter the statement that its own systems were not breached.

These sources contain researcher observations and platform responses, not penetration testing by this site. They show that prompt-data risk involves the inference providers and routing actually used; a retention label alone does not establish the security of the entire processing chain. We provide no attack-reproduction procedure and do not infer a wider affected scope than the material supports.
