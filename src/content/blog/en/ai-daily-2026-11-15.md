## November 12: Baidu opens APIs for two application models

CNR's November 12 conference report confirms that Baidu released its ERNIE deep-thinking and facial-microexpression application models, made them available on Qianfan, and opened APIs to enterprise customers and partners. These reasoning and portrait-video products should be distinguished from the ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview foundation model announced at the same event.

The archived Baidu product page describes ERNIE-Light-DT-1.0 in terms of BSA sparse attention and history compression. ERNIE-Wizshot-ME can generate 4–15-second, 1080p videos of realistic-looking people, with a listed price of approximately RMB 3.5 for a five-second video. These are the vendor's specifications and prices at the archive date, not our measurements or current quotations.

For integration teams, the two APIs address different inputs, outputs and billing units. The sources establish the announcement and access scope, but do not establish superiority in a shared business benchmark. We did not call the APIs or compare video quality.

## November 13: ChatGPT Wallet retains approval for each purchase

On November 13, OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. Initial access through dots was limited to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 or older with eligible US-issued cards; the announcement did not establish worldwide availability.

Under the documented flow, users review the items, total and selected card, then approve the purchase through a security check such as facial or fingerprint authentication. The payment network subsequently supplies a token in place of the actual card details. This binds the agent's payment action to a specific authorization without giving the agent or merchant the user's actual card number.

Fully delegated purchases within a preset budget remained a future direction. OpenAI had already announced checkout in chat in September 2025, so this should not be described as ChatGPT's first shopping capability. We checked the published process, not payment success rates or bank compatibility.

## November 13–14 reporting: Microsoft disruption reaches AI connectors

The Associated Press reported on November 13 that Microsoft's outage disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot, with some Hong Kong banks also affected. OpenAI and Anthropic reported difficulties connecting to Microsoft 365 tools, showing how a dependency can affect multiple AI products.

A November 14 National Business Daily report relayed Microsoft's update from November 13 local time: consumer services had recovered, while Microsoft 365 enterprise services were showing recovery and required processing of queued email. Recovery of consumer services did not establish that every enterprise system was operating normally.

The AP attributed the incident explanation to Microsoft: a technical issue during an upgrade following a recent network change, with no indication of a cyberattack or AI agents causing it. Those are attributed statements at the reporting date. We did not obtain internal incident records or reproduce the outage.
