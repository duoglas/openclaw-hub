## November 21: Downloads and platform token volume measure different things

A November 21 People's Daily report, citing China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, states that cumulative worldwide downloads of Chinese open models exceeded 10 billion. It separately reports 119.1 trillion tokens of usage for Chinese models on OpenRouter during November 9–15.

The first measure is cumulative downloads; the second is usage on one platform during one week. Neither is a count of unique users, and they do not share an observation window. They indicate distribution and usage activity without directly establishing global market share, paid revenue or customer retention.

The report also distinguishes open model weights from publicly available software source code. For teams considering self-hosting or fine-tuning, availability and licensing remain separate questions. We cite the published statistics without independently recounting platform traffic or reviewing each model's license.

## In the edition's archive: AURA weights are limited to noncommercial use

The Kyutai AURA-1.3B model card archived on November 22 describes a Wan2.1-based, audio-driven avatar model that turns a portrait and speech stream into video chunks. Kyutai's project article is explicitly dated November 20, 2026. That is the article date; the available material does not independently establish when the weights first became available.

The card reports 20.7 frames per second on one H100 for the paper's optimized pipeline, versus about 16 frames per second for the compiled reference code. These figures concern different implementations and do not establish default performance for the reference code or consumer hardware.

Both merged and LoRA weights use CC BY-NC 4.0 for noncommercial use only; the Wan2.1 VAE and text encoder retain their own licenses. Downloadable weights do not imply free commercial use. We did not run real-time inference or verify whether any third party's use meets the licensing conditions.

## November 20: MedChemBench results do not establish drug discovery

OpenAI's November 20 research article describes MedChemBench as 99 problems drawn from published research, covering 117 papers and patents and 761 molecular structures. The company reports that GPT-6.2 Astra earned 84.5% of the available credit at the xhigh reasoning setting.

OpenAI's June 3 GPT-Rosalind article had already introduced MedChemBench. The later publication adds research detail and model results; it is not the benchmark's first appearance, and its percentage score is not a drug-development success rate.

The source explicitly says prior model exposure to the public material cannot be ruled out. Each response was a single attempt without tools, feedback or new experiments. The results do not establish that models can discover new drugs or improve real laboratory decisions. We did not conduct chemical experiments or independently reproduce the evaluation.
